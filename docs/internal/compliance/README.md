## Why these matter

**1. Service agreement template.** Every client needs to sign before work starts. Without it, you have no enforceable scope, no IP ownership terms, no liability cap, no payment recourse. Even pilot clients sign one (with $0 fees in Exhibit A).

**2. BAA template.** HIPAA requires every business associate that touches Protected Health Information to have a signed BAA with the covered entity. Phase 1 for therapy/dental is intentionally non-PHI, so a BAA is not legally required for Phase 1 itself — but Phase 2+ requires one, and most healthcare clients will ask for one upfront as a trust signal even in Phase 1. Having a template ready means you sign in days, not weeks.

**3. E&O + cyber insurance.** Errors & Omissions covers professional liability (claim that your service caused financial harm). Cyber covers data breaches, ransomware response, third-party claims arising from cyber incidents. Both are standard for AI freelancers and table stakes for healthcare-adjacent work. Embracing Minds will not ask to see your certificate of insurance for the pilot, but a paid client likely will.

## Execution order

1. **Read all three documents and the inline disclaimers** before sending to anyone.
2. **Find a Texas-licensed business attorney** with tech-services + healthcare experience. Two practical paths:
   - Avvo or Texas State Bar Find-A-Lawyer with filter "business law" + "Texas" + "HIPAA" experience
   - LegalShield or RocketLawyer for cheaper first-pass redlines (under $500); use a senior attorney for the final read
   - The two healthcare-tech specialty firms in Texas worth a cold email: Hall Render (national, Austin office) and Norton Rose Fulbright (Houston). They're more expensive than solo practitioners but understand the BAA + HB 300 stack natively.
3. **Send both templates to the attorney** with the engagement-context note in each file's preamble. Budget $1,000–$3,000 for the redline pass on both documents combined. Allow 1–2 weeks turnaround.
4. **Run the insurance shopping process in parallel** per the spec doc. Quotes return in 3–5 business days.
5. **Bind insurance before signing the first paying client.** Use the certificate of insurance (COI) as a sales asset.

## What this pack does NOT cover

- **State LLC formation.** Forming ClearAutomations LLC in Texas is its own separate step. Cost: $300 filing fee + ~$100 registered agent yearly. Resources: Texas Secretary of State `direct.sos.state.tx.us`. Self-serve via IncFile or Northwest Registered Agent if you prefer guided.
- **EIN / federal tax.** After LLC formation, get an EIN at irs.gov (free, instant). Open a business bank account in CA's name.
- **Sales tax registration.** Texas does not impose sales tax on services in most cases. Confirm with CPA.
- **Operating agreement.** Even single-member LLCs benefit from one. Attorney can draft alongside the MSA review.
- **Trademark / brand protection.** Out of scope here; if the CA brand grows, file a trademark application via USPTO TEAS.
