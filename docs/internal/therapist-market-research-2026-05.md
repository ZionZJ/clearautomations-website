## 1. US therapy market sizing (2024–2026)

### Total industry revenue and growth

- **US Mental Health & Substance Abuse Clinics (outpatient) industry revenue: $40.8B (2026)**, five-year CAGR 4.2%, projected 2026 growth 3.0% ([IBISWorld report 1568, 2026](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/mental-health-substance-abuse-clinics/1568/)).
- **Inpatient Mental Health & Substance Abuse Centers: $31.4B (2026)**, 5.9% five-year CAGR ([IBISWorld report 1597, 2026](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/mental-health-substance-abuse-centers/1597/)).
- **US outpatient mental health enterprises: 16,056 (2024), +4.1% year-over-year** ([IBISWorld Number of Businesses, 1568](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/mental-health-substance-abuse-clinics/1568/)).
- **Broader behavioral health market projected at $166.9B by 2034** (Precedence Research, 2024) — captures outpatient + inpatient + digital channels combined.

### Clinician workforce (BLS, May 2024 OES + 2024–2034 projections)

| Role | 2024 employment | 2024–2034 projection | Annual openings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Substance abuse / behavioral disorder / mental health counselors (LPC bucket) | 483,500 | +17% (much faster than average) | ~48,300/yr |
| Marriage and family therapists (LMFT) | 77,800 | +13% | ~7,700/yr |
| Psychologists | 204,300 | +6% | ~12,900/yr |
| Social workers (all categories, includes LCSW) | 810,900 | +6% | — |

Sources: BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook — [Counselors](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/substance-abuse-behavioral-disorder-and-mental-health-counselors.htm), [LMFTs](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/marriage-and-family-therapists.htm), [Psychologists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/psychologists.htm), [Social Workers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/social-workers.htm).

The BLS social-worker total (810,900) bundles all categories. The 2024 Social Work Workforce Study estimates **~223,800 licensed master's-level social workers (the LCSW-equivalent population)** nationally ([Social Work Workforce Study 2024 Executive Summary](https://swcensus.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Social-Work-Workforce-Study-Executive-Summary-for-Regulators.pdf)).

**Combined addressable clinician pool (LPC + LMFT + LCSW + psychologist):** ~990K licensed mental-health-providing clinicians nationally.

**Headline trend:** [BLS Economics Daily (Sept 2024)](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2024/strong-growth-projected-in-mental-health-related-employment.htm) — mental-health-related employment is projected to grow about 3× the rate of all US jobs through 2034.

## 2. Group-practice segment — the CA target

### EHR + practice size

- **SimplePractice serves 225,000+ practitioners; 88% of customers are organizations with under 50 employees** ([Enlyft profile](https://enlyft.com/tech/products/simplepractice)).
- SimplePractice is positioned as the polished EHR for 1–5-clinician practices (Crown Counseling EHR review, 2024). The CA target of 2–15 clinicians sits squarely in the SimplePractice / TherapyNotes core user base.

### Group-practice compensation and economics

- **Contractor fee splits dominate:** 70% of group practices use fee-split contractor compensation; 70/30 or 60/40 splits are most common (Heard 2024 Financial State of Private Practice).
- **Average revenue benchmarks (Heard 2024 tax-year data):**
  - Sole proprietor: median revenue ~$91,000/yr per clinician (≈$5,500/mo take-home after expenses)
  - Group practice (S-Corp): average revenue **$179,003** with average net income **$115,250** (up 1.5% year-over-year); expenses up 11.2% year-over-year
  - All practices combined: average revenue $127,631 (+2.7% YoY)
  - Source: [Heard 2024 income benchmarks](https://www.joinheard.com/articles/therapist-income-benchmarks-what-to-expect-in-private-practice) and [Heard 2025 income report](https://www.joinheard.com/how-much-did-therapists-make-in-2025)
- **Average session rates (Heard, 2024–2025):**
  - Private pay across all license types: **$159**
  - Insurance-reimbursed: **$111** (36% lower)
  - Cash-pay-only therapists averaged **$157/session**
- **Net margins:** Therapy practices average **20–40% net profit margin** ([Traktion Accounting, 2024](https://traktionaccounting.com/average-profit-of-a-therapy-practice/)).

### EM-anchored revenue math (a 5-clinician practice baseline)

Embracing Minds is a 5-clinician, insurance-accepting practice (Aetna, Cigna, BCBS, UHC) with self-pay sliding-scale through Stephanie LPC-A. Applying the Heard 2024 benchmarks:

- Estimated annual revenue at 5 clinicians × ~$120 average session × ~250 sessions/mo per clinician × 12 mo ≈ **$1.8M gross** (very high), OR
- More realistic: per-clinician avg revenue per Heard group-practice S-Corp benchmark of $179K × 5 ≈ **$900K** gross (the practice keeps maybe $200–$360K net at 20–40% margin)
- Self-pay rates ($105–$125 individual, $90–$155 couples per the EM profile) sit slightly below the Heard $159 private-pay average — typical for the Houston market given lower regional cost-of-living

These ranges are wide because session-volume and insurance-mix vary substantially. The mid-point (~$900K gross / $250K net) is the working assumption for ROI math below.

## 3. Operational baselines — what Phase 1 actually moves

### No-show rate (the single highest-leverage metric)

- **Outpatient therapy: 20–30% no-show rate is typical**; psychiatry averages ~23%; substance use disorder runs 30–50% ([Curogram, 2024](https://curogram.com/blog/mental-health/mental-health-appointment-no-shows)).
- Range cited: **20–50%** depending on setting; **more than 2× the ~18% all-medical average** (concordant with [PMC11231932](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11231932/) and the [Mend whitepaper, 2024](https://mend.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/eliminating-no-shows-mental-health.pdf)).

This is the dollar-impact line. For a 5-clinician practice doing ~$900K gross with a 22% no-show baseline, every percentage point recovered ≈ ~$9K/yr in salvaged session revenue. A 15–25% relative no-show reduction (Phase 1 target) translates to **~3–5 percentage points absolute**, or **~$27K–$45K/yr recovered session revenue**.

### Response time to inquiries

- **SimplePractice's published guidance to providers: reply within 48 hours** ([SimplePractice Inquiries documentation](https://support.simplepractice.com/hc/en-us/articles/33726366744589)). This is the *target*, not the observed average.
- **No published 2024 benchmark of actual therapist inquiry response times was located** — *industry-estimate, no source confirmed* that typical small-practice intake response runs 12–48 hours when staff are present and 1–3+ business days for weekend/after-hours.

### The lead-response-time to conversion correlation

- **MIT / Oldroyd 2007 study** (Sloan School + InsideSales.com, 15,000+ leads across 100+ companies): leads contacted within 5 minutes are **21× more likely to qualify** than leads contacted after 30 minutes; **odds of making contact at all drop ~100×** between the 5-minute and 30-minute mark ([MIT Lead Response Management Study PDF](https://25649.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na2.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf)).
- **HBR follow-up (Oldroyd et al. 2011):** average B2B first response was 42–47 hours; first responders win ~78% of deals; only ~1% of B2B companies respond in under 5 minutes.
- **Healthcare-specific data (InfluxMD, 2025):** leads contacted within 5 minutes are ~10× more likely to convert vs the typical 47-hour industry response. Concordant with the broader Oldroyd finding.
- **Counter-evidence:** the "60-second / 391% conversion lift" specific figure has been challenged in some 2024–2025 re-analyses. The 5-minute window is well-supported; the exact percent-lift figure varies by sample. Use ranges, not point estimates.

### What this means for the Phase 1 pitch

The 21× and 10× figures are headline-grade and well-sourced. The honest framing: "Our system responds inside 2 minutes, 24/7. Industry data says that's the difference between a 21× conversion advantage and the baseline 47-hour response curve." We do not need to over-quote any specific lift figure; the order-of-magnitude difference is the argument.

### Bilingual demand

- **44.9M US residents age 5+ speak Spanish at home — ~14% of the US population** ([US Census ACS 2024 S1601](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSST1Y2024.S1601?q=Language+Spoken+at+Home)).
- **Hispanic share of US population: ~18%**.
- **Spanish-speaking clinician supply: under 6% of US psychologists** can deliver services in Spanish ([APA Monitor, "Spanish-speaking psychologists in demand"](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2018/06/spanish-speaking)). Mental health counselors are 15.3% Hispanic ([Data USA](https://datausa.io/profile/soc/mental-health-counselors)) but Hispanic ethnicity does not equal Spanish clinical competence.
- **Hispanic-American mental healthcare gap projected at $500M annually by 2030** ([SimplePractice blog, 2024](https://www.simplepractice.com/blog/hispanic-american-mental-health-care-gap/)).

A practice that genuinely offers Spanish clinical services in Texas captures supply-demand asymmetry that the rest of the market structurally cannot.

## 4. Texas-specific submarket

### Demographics

- **Texas total population: ~30.5M (2024)**.
- **Hispanic/Latino share of Texas: 40.2%** (US Census 2023 ACS 1-year; [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2023/06/21/census-texas-hispanic-population-demographics/); [Every Texan 2024](https://everytexan.org/2024/10/08/data-brief-hispanic-heritage-month-2024/)).
- Hispanic share has been the **single largest demographic group in Texas since July 2022**.
- **Implication:** a Texas-first market that is 40% Hispanic, vs the 18% US average, sharply elevates the relevance of Spanish bilingual intake automation. The Phase 1 Spanish lane is not a nice-to-have in Texas; it is a load-bearing capability.

### Texas workforce shortage

- **246 of 254 Texas counties (~97%) are HRSA-designated mental health professional shortage areas (HPSAs)** ([Rural Health Information Hub, Jan 2026](https://www.ruralhealthinfo.org/charts/7); [Texas DSHS Shortage Area Designations](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/center-health-statistics/texas-primary-care-office-tpco/shortage-area-designations)).
- **Harris County (Houston): avg 300 days to fill a psychiatrist vacancy in 2024** ([Rice Kinder Institute, 2024](https://kinder.rice.edu/urbanedge/harris-county-contends-shortage-mental-health-workers-despite-landmark-state-investment)).
- **Texas median wages (BLS May 2024 OES):** LPC ~$59,190 median; LCSW ~$60,060 median.
- **LCSW Texas private-practice average (ZipRecruiter, Dec 2025):** $87,723/yr, IQR $67,100–$104,300.
- **Total active Texas-licensed LPC / LCSW / LMFT headcounts:** publicly available via the Texas Behavioral Health Executive Council license verification system at [bhec.texas.gov](https://bhec.texas.gov/) but not surfaced as aggregate counts in standard reports. *Industry-estimate, no source confirmed* — likely 20,000–30,000 active LPCs and 12,000–18,000 active LCSWs based on Texas population share of BLS national totals.

### Texas HB 300 — regulatory implication

Texas Medical Records Privacy Act (HB 300) layers state requirements on top of HIPAA. The four practitioner-relevant tightenings:

1. **Broader "covered entity" definition.** Covers anyone who assembles, collects, analyzes, uses, evaluates, stores, or transmits PHI of a Texas resident — including out-of-state vendors. Many tech vendors that escape HIPAA Business Associate status are on the hook in Texas.
2. **15-business-day EHR access requirement** (HIPAA allows 30 days + one 30-day extension).
3. **Mandatory workforce training** on state + federal PHI law within the statutory onboarding window, with documented employee attestation.
4. **Dual-penalty exposure.** A single breach can trigger HIPAA and HB 300 penalties separately. Texas civil penalties scale to $1.5M/yr per category.

Sources: [HIPAA Journal, "What is Texas HB 300?"](https://www.hipaajournal.com/what-is-texas-hb-300/); [Compliancy Group HB 300 + HIPAA](https://compliancy-group.com/texas-hb-300/); [Accountable HQ HB 300 explained](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/texas-hb-300-texas-medical-privacy-act-explained-requirements-training-and-penalties).

**Sales-collateral wedge:** any AI intake automation marketed into Texas must clear HIPAA + HB 300. Workforce training and out-of-state vendor provisions are where most competitors fall short. The Phase 1 product (intentionally non-PHI) is unusually defensible on this dimension because it avoids the data category entirely.

## 5. Telehealth in mental health

Mental-health telehealth is the segment where pandemic-era adoption was most permanent. The data:

- **Mental-health-related telehealth visits were 47% of all telehealth visits in 2020, rising to 58% by 2023** ([CIVHC analysis, Feb 2025](https://civhc.org/2025/02/11/new-telehealth-analysis-shows-sustained-demand-for-mental-and-behavioral-health-services/)).
- **~55% of mental health appointments are conducted via video** as of early 2024 ([Annals of Internal Medicine via Washington Post, Feb 2024](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wellness/2024/02/12/mental-health-online-telemedicine-therapy/)).
- **Behavioral health is the stickiest telehealth segment post-pandemic** — rates declined slightly (27.2 visits/1,000 in 2021 → 24.2 in 2024) but remain dramatically above 2019 baseline ([ConnectWithCare/RAND analysis, Mar 2025](https://connectwithcare.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025.03.05.25323449.full_.pdf)).
- **~28% of mental health outpatients use telehealth at all** (PNAS Nexus 2024 EHR cohort) — the lower bound; the 55–58% figure is share of appointment volume.

**Implication for the response-time pitch:** Texas telehealth-capable practices effectively serve a state-wide patient pool (Embracing Minds is licensed statewide). Wider geography means broader inquiry-arrival hours (after-work in El Paso = 7pm in Dallas), and a higher share of after-hours intakes that 24/7 automated response will catch. This compounds the response-time value proposition.

## 6. Insurance vs self-pay landscape

- **~75% of therapists accept some insurance**, but the trend is toward dropping panels ([Health Affairs Scholar, Sept 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11412241/); [NPR coverage, Dec 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/12/17/nx-s1-5230469/an-increasing-number-of-mental-health-practitioners-do-not-take-insurance)).
- **Psychologists specifically: 66% in-network with at least one insurer, 34% no-insurance** ([APA 2024 Practitioner Pulse Survey](https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/practitioner/2024/practitioner-pulse-2024-full-report.pdf)).
- **Mixed-model dominance:** Heard 2024 found 8% fully insurance-based, 32% fully private-pay, **60% hybrid**. Among the 27% cash-pay-only, average rate was $157/session.
- **Reasons psychologists drop insurance (APA 2024):** 82% insufficient reimbursement; 62% admin burden; 52% payment reliability.
- **Regional spread:** highest insurance acceptance ND 90.5% / NE 87.1%; lowest DC 41.0% / TN 51.5% / CA 55.4%. **Texas sits closer to the national median** (specific Texas percentage not surfaced; estimable as 65–75%).
- **Self-pay rates (2024):** Heard average $143–$157; SimplePractice national average ~$100–$250 with midpoint near $140; high-cost metros up to $250+; Open Path Collective sliding-scale at $40–$70 individual / $40–$80 couples ([Open Path pricing](https://openpathcollective.org/pricing-and-eligibility-for-affordable-therapy/)).
- **Five-year session-rate inflation: ~13%** ([SimplePractice, 2024](https://www.simplepractice.com/blog/average-therapy-session-rate-by-state/)).

**EM positioning:** Embracing Minds is hybrid (Aetna/Cigna/BCBS/UHC + self-pay sliding scale). That posture matches 60% of the US therapy practice market. Phase 1 routing logic that handles both insurance and self-pay inquiries with appropriate Q&A is genuinely market-fit.

## 8. Phase 1 ROI math (the synthesis)

Building from the data above, here is the Phase 1 ROI case for the EM-sized therapy practice (~5 clinicians, ~$900K gross revenue, 20–30% no-show baseline, hybrid insurance + self-pay).

### Recoverable revenue per Year 1

**Lever 1 — No-show recovery (highest-leverage):**

- Baseline no-show rate: 22% (mid-range industry)
- Phase 1 target: 15–25% relative reduction = 3–5 percentage points absolute
- At ~250 sessions/month per clinician × 5 clinicians = 1,250 sessions/mo (loose)
- Per percentage point recovered: ~12.5 sessions/mo × $120 avg session × 12 = ~$18K/yr
- 3-point reduction = **~$54K/yr** | 5-point reduction = **~$90K/yr**
- Realistic mid-case: **~$50K–$70K/yr in salvaged session revenue**

**Lever 2 — Lead-to-consult lift (medium-leverage):**

- Baseline: typical small-practice response 12–48 hours during business hours, 1–3 days after-hours
- Phase 1: under-2-minute response 24/7
- Conservative conversion lift estimate: 15–30% on the leads that would have ghosted (per MIT/Oldroyd: 21× qualification odds inside the 5-minute window)
- For a practice that gets, say, 30 inquiries/month at a 30% baseline consult-conversion rate: from 9 consults to ~11–12 consults/month
- 2-3 net new clients/month × $120 × 8-session retention = **~$23K–$35K/yr net new revenue**

**Lever 3 — Spanish-bilingual lane (Texas-specific):**

- Texas is 40% Hispanic vs 18% US average. EM's existing bilingual capability is currently invisible to Spanish-search traffic.
- Per the Spanish-supply mismatch (under 6% of psychologists vs 14% of US Spanish-at-home households), the lane captures untapped demand structurally.
- *Industry-estimate, no source confirmed:* incremental 5–15% lead volume from properly-surfaced Spanish lane in a Texas market.
- Conservative: **~$10K–$25K/yr in incremental Spanish-driven revenue** (net of clinician capacity).

**Total Year 1 Phase 1 revenue impact: ~$80K–$130K** for a 5-clinician Texas practice. This is a model, not a guarantee.

### Phase 1 cost (Year 1)

- ClearAutomations setup + retainer: $4,500 + ($400 × 12) = **$9,300**
- Vendor stack: $70–$120/mo × 12 = **$840–$1,440**
- **Total Year 1 Phase 1 cost: ~$10,000–$11,000**

### Net Year 1 ROI

**Roughly 7×–13× return on the Phase 1 investment** in salvaged + new + Spanish-driven revenue, before any Phase 2/3 layer. The math is conservative on the lift side and uses mid-range industry baselines. It does not assume any clinician capacity expansion.

### When this ROI doesn't apply

Honest flags for when the math breaks:

- Practices with no-show rates already under 12% (Phase 1 has less room to recover)
- Practices with inquiry volume under ~10/mo (the response-time lever has less material flow)
- Practices entirely on Headway / Alma where intake doesn't touch the practice's own surface
- Practices whose bottleneck is genuinely clinician capacity (not lead flow); for those, Phase 1 surfaces the bottleneck but doesn't solve it

## 10. Verdict on the existing demo script

I read [client-demo-script.md](./../industries/therapist-practice/client-demo-script.md). It's a thin 70-line technical walkthrough that adequately covers what each workflow does, but it's insufficient for selling. Group B refresh needed for the Loom v1 script.

**What the current script does well:**

- Lists each workflow (W01 through W04 + W07 + simulated W05/W06)
- Honest opening disclaimer about fictional data + simulated Phase 2/3
- Clean compliance-aware language (no implied PHI claims)

**What the current script lacks (must add for Loom v1):**

1. **Hook / value-prop opening.** Currently jumps straight to W01 demo. Needs a 30-second opener that frames *why* — the 21× response-time stat, the 20–30% no-show problem, the after-hours leak.
2. **Voice agent demo.** The most impressive part of the product is currently absent from the script. The Retell agent walking through a synthetic inquiry, hitting the crisis branch, hitting the Spanish lane — this is the demo's emotional payoff.
3. **Crisis-branch demo.** A single synthetic Spanish-language crisis-keyword payload that triggers the 988 + ER + DV-hotline verbatim script, then disconnects, then surfaces the SMS alert. Three-second moment that closes the trust gap.
4. **Spanish lane demo.** Texas-specific. The bilingual agent path is a load-bearing differentiator and is currently invisible.
5. **Pricing close.** Current script ends with "the first paid step is a therapist-practice audit." Needs to land the actual Phase 1 + 2 + 3 framing, the vendor pass-through transparency, and the typical Year 1 ROI math (per Section 8 above).
6. **"What we set up for [your practice]"** close — personalization handoff that turns the demo into a discovery conversation.

**Recommendation:** Queue a Group B refresh of `client-demo-script.md` post-cutover. Use this brief's Section 8 (ROI math) and Section 9 (talking points) as content building blocks. Don't refresh during the EM cutover window (per the strategic-review-2026-05 constraint).
